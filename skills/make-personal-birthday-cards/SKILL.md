# Make personal birthday cards

Start with the story the sender would tell at the birthday table. Find one object the recipient would recognise and one thing it can reveal. A teacher who used to spring a musical joke on a class suggests an old computer disk with a straight-faced label. Reserve the punchline for the moment the disk goes in. A private memory can inform the design without appearing in the public card.

## Design the front and the moment inside

- Match the delivery to the recipient. A remote birthday today calls for a link that works immediately, with a finished greeting even if the sender skips the editor. Make a print edition when the sender wants paper or both formats.
- Design the front as one object. Give it believable materials, a legible title, and one or two details that belong to this person. Make the artwork and typography hold up as a still image before adding movement. Set exact names and inscriptions in typeset text.
- Use the sender's actual words for a supplied message. For a suggested message, write one concrete affectionate line and let the sender replace it. A name, a birthday and a personal history deserve different decisions about what belongs on a publicly shareable front.
- Use the exact requested recording or performance. Check artist, arrangement and destination. Use the artist's official player or link. When the player refuses to load, keep a direct play link visible. Preserve the joke in the preview title, image, alt text and caption.

## Make the requested editions

**Paper.** Compose for the physical size. Leave pen room and comfortable trim margins. Use one high-contrast QR with a four-module quiet zone pointing to the finished card or approved recording. Make the same destination tappable on screen. Render at print size, check the page count, decode the rendered QR and follow its destination. Put the actual card in the sender's hands when a printable file is requested.

**Phone.** Build one meaningful gesture with a visible tap equivalent. A disk, envelope or cover can move from ready to opening to revealed and back. Keep loading short, support keyboard and reduced motion, and preserve a useful no-script path. Start audio from a gesture, and retain a separate play control for devices that defer playback. Crop artwork for small screens and test the inscription, controls and reveal at narrow phone widths.

**Sender's note.** Keep the writing link distinct from the recipient link. Offer touch handwriting and a typed equivalent when personalisation is requested. A compact URL fragment can carry a short note without a database. Keep the fragment in the final share URL, remove editor parameters and make the note readable again in a fresh visit. Tell the sender that anyone with that link can read the note if privacy matters.

## Ship and check

1. Preserve the accepted artwork when moving between hosts. For project Pages, use the entire deployed subpath in the canonical URL, social preview, QR and share links. Use relative paths for assets and test under that same subpath.
2. Verify the sealed cover, tap and drag, recording fallback, note round trip and replay using the editions actually built. A print mockup, a share sheet or an embed URL alone cannot establish playback or delivery.
3. Keep the repository link separate from the card link. Give the sender the ready-to-send address first, then the handwriting link when available. Sending the message stays with the sender unless explicitly requested.
